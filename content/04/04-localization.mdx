## 1. Why Localization Is a Perceptual Problem

<Callout type="success" title="Core idea">
The auditory system never sees a spatial map; it reconstructs direction from microsecond timing, small level differences, and spectral coloration between the ears.
</Callout>

Sound localization is **inferred** from interaural disparities and the filtering applied by the head and outer ears. Two physical constraints drive this perceptual work:

- The ears sit only ~18–20 cm apart.
- Sound arrives at both ears within a few hundred microseconds.

Consequently, the brain relies on **microsecond timing differences**, **small level disparities**, and **frequency-dependent coloring** to decode direction.

---

## 2. Binaural Hearing and the Head as an Obstacle

When a source shifts off-center:
- the head introduces a **path length difference** between ears.
- the head casts an **acoustic shadow**, especially at high frequencies.

<ImageSection src="/images/chapter04/subchapter03-2.1.png" caption="Head-induced delay and shadowing that create localization cues" />

<div className="table-wrapper"><table><thead><tr><th>Cue</th><th>Origin</th><th>Dominant band</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Interaural Time Difference (ITD)</td><td>Arrival-delay caused by head width</td><td>Below ~1.5 kHz</td></tr><tr><td>Interaural Level Difference (ILD)</td><td>High-frequency shadowing</td><td>Above ~2 kHz</td></tr><tr><td>Spectral Cues</td><td>Pinna-induced notches and peaks</td><td>Typically >4 kHz</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch out">
The cues overlap, so the brain balances them rather than trusting a single measurement across all frequencies.
</Callout>

These effects give rise to the principal binaural cues (ITD and ILD) plus the spectral fingerprints that flag elevation and front/back contrasts.

---

## 3. Interaural Time Difference (ITD)

<Callout type="info" title="When ITD leads">
ITD dominates below ~1.5 kHz, where wavelengths exceed head width and phase locking is robust.
</Callout>

**ITD** is the difference in arrival time between the left and right ears. For a source at azimuth angle <MathFormula inline>{"\\theta"}</MathFormula>:

<MathFormula>
{"\\mathrm{ITD} \\approx \\frac{d}{c} \\sin\\theta"}
</MathFormula>

where:
- <MathFormula inline>{"d"}</MathFormula> is the interaural distance.
- <MathFormula inline>{"c"}</MathFormula> is the speed of sound.

### 3.1 Key properties

- ITD excels at **low frequencies** where wavelengths are long.
- Maximum ITD ≈ **600–700 µs** for an average human head.
- Dominant cue below ~1.5 kHz, becoming less reliable with high-frequency tones.

---

## 4. Interaural Level Difference (ILD)

**ILD** arises because the head attenuates high-frequency energy that reaches the far ear.

<ImageSection src="/images/chapter04/subchapter03-4.1.webp" caption="High-frequency shadowing creates level differences" />

<Callout type="info" title="High-frequency focus">
ILD grows with frequency as the head shadow becomes stronger, so it carries most of the spatial detail for bright sounds.
</Callout>

- ILD increases with frequency due to stronger head shadowing.
- It is negligible at very low frequencies because the head diffracts long wavelengths.
- Can exceed **20 dB** above ~5 kHz, giving a potent spatial cue.

ILD is therefore the dominant localization cue for high-frequency content.

---

## 5. Duplex Theory of Localization

<Callout type="info" title="Rule of thumb">
Use ITD for low-frequency content and ILD for high-frequency content, but remember the transition zone around 2 kHz where both cues contribute.
</Callout>

- **Low frequencies** → ITD dominates.
- **High frequencies** → ILD dominates.

This duplex theory captures why horizontal-plane localization works so reliably for broadband sounds: timing and level cues cooperate across the spectrum.

---

## 6. Spectral Cues and the Role of the Pinna

Localization extends beyond left/right. The **pinna (outer ear)** introduces direction-dependent frequency shaping.

- Elevation and front/back position change how the pinna filters incoming waves.
- Spectral notches and peaks shift with direction, providing high-frequency tags.

<ImageSection src="/images/chapter04/subchapter03-6.1.png" caption="Pinna-induced spectral notches encode vertical and front/back information" />

These spectral profiles help resolve ambiguities that binaural cues alone cannot, especially for sources on the vertical plane or in front/back.

---

## 7. Head-Related Transfer Functions (HRTFs)

The combined filtering from the head, torso, shoulders, and pinnae is captured by the **Head-Related Transfer Function (HRTF)**.

An HRTF maps a source direction to the left and right ear signals. They are:
- direction dependent.
- listener specific.
- frequency dependent.

<Callout type="info" title="Why HRTFs matter">
HRTFs are the physical blueprint for binaural rendering—playing back signals filtered with the right HRTF convinces the brain a source is coming from that direction.
</Callout>

These functions form the foundation for spatial audio and virtual sound placement.

---

## 8. Front–Back Confusions and Cone of Confusion

Some source positions yield identical ITD and ILD values, creating the **cone of confusion**.

- Front and back sources on the cone share the same binaural cues.
- Elevation and spectral cues supplement binaural measurements.
- Head movements break the ambiguity by changing the cue pattern over time.

<Callout type="warning" title="Ambiguity">
Pure tones on the cone of confusion are almost impossible to place without additional cues; broadband sounds or motion become essential.
</Callout>

Listeners resolve these ambiguities with spectral cues, pinna filtering, and deliberate head turns.

---

## 9. Limits of Localization Accuracy

Human localization performance depends on:
- frequency content.
- signal duration.
- reverberation.
- listener experience.

Typical performance: horizontal localization can reach **1–2°** accuracy under ideal conditions, while vertical localization remains significantly less precise.

---

## 10. Summary

- Localization relies on **binaural differences** and **spectral filtering**.
- ITD dominates at low frequencies, ILD at high frequencies.
- The pinna provides elevation and front/back cues.
- HRTFs describe direction-dependent ear signals.
- Localization is powerful but not free from ambiguity.
