## 1. Why Sound Power Is Introduced

Microphones record sound pressure at one spot, so the reading depends on distance, reflections, and room geometry. Two listeners can hear the same source quite differently even though the source itself is unchanged.

<Callout type="success" title="Key Idea">
  Sound pressure is a field quantity tied to your location; sound power is a
  source quantity that stays constant regardless of where you stand.
</Callout>

Sound power integrates the total acoustic energy flux through a surface enclosing the source, isolating the intrinsic strength of the emitter.

---

## 2. Physical Definition of Sound Power

Sound power equals the surface integral of sound intensity over any closed surface surrounding the source:

<MathFormula> {"W = \\iint_S I\\, dA"} </MathFormula>

Here:

- **W** is sound power in watts
- **I** is sound intensity (W/m²)
- **S** is any closed surface fully enclosing the source

<Callout type="info" title="Why the surface can move">
  As long as the surface wraps the source without cutting through it, the
  integral gives the same result—the total acoustic flux stays constant while
  intensity redistributes across different areas.
</Callout>

This shields the sound power from the vagaries of room acoustics or microphone placement.

## 4. Sound Power Level

Sound power spans many orders of magnitude, so we express it logarithmically:

<MathFormula> {"L_W = 10\\log_{10}\\left(\\frac{W}{W_0}\\right)"} </MathFormula>

with the reference power: <MathFormula inline> {"W_0 = 10^{-12}\\,\\mathrm{W}"} </MathFormula>

Using this reference keeps typical sound power levels within a workable numerical range.

## 6. Why Sound Power Is Used for Source Ratings

Sound power ratings let manufacturers and regulators compare devices objectively because they stay stable across environments. Labels or specifications often appear for:

<ul className="list-disc">
  <li>household appliances</li>
  <li>ventilation systems</li>
  <li>industrial machinery</li>
  <li>construction equipment</li>
</ul>

In many regions, Europe is a prominent example. Sound power labeling is required to guarantee transparent comparisons.

---

## 7. Relation to the Point Source Model

For a source behaving like a point emitter in a free field, intensity at distance \(r\) satisfies: <MathFormula inline> {"W = 4\\pi r^2 I"} </MathFormula>

That equation shows how sound power and intensity connect when you can control the measurement environment. Reliable sound power estimates from pressure require:

i. Anechoic or hemi-anechoic chambers  
 ii. Standardized test environments  
 iii. Protocols that suppress reflections

Outside those setups, pressure-only readings cannot determine sound power accurately.

## 8. Optional Numerical Insight

The Python demo below converts sound power levels to watts so you can observe how dB differences translate into real acoustic energy changes.

<div className="python-demo">
  <PythonDemo
    id="source-power-conversion"
    title="Sound power level to watts"
    description="Convert sound power levels into absolute watts using the reference power to see how the emitted energy grows."
    defaultCode={String.raw`
import numpy as np

LW = np.array([60, 80, 100]) # sound power levels in dB
W0 = 1e-12 # reference power (W)

W = W0 \* 10\*\*(LW/10)

for lw, w in zip(LW, W):
print(f"LW = {lw} dB → W = {w:.2e} W")
`}
/>

</div>

This calculation shows how relatively modest increases in sound power level correspond to large gains in emitted acoustic energy.

---

## 9. Summary

Sound power records the total acoustic energy emitted per second and remains independent of measurement conditions. Expressing it as a sound power level makes source comparisons manageable and underpins labeling and regulations.

Understanding this invariant quantity clears the way for exploring directivity and measurement strategies in the next chapter.
